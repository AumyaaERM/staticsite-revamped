## From cost center to strategic innovation engine

The Global Capability Center (GCC) model has undergone its most significant transformation in two decades. What began as a cost-arbitrage play — offshoring back-office processes to reduce operational spend — has evolved into something fundamentally different: AI-powered innovation hubs that sit at the center of enterprise strategy, digital transformation, and competitive differentiation.

The numbers reflect this shift clearly. India alone hosts over **1,800 active GCCs** employing nearly **2 million professionals**, with GCC revenue projected to exceed **USD 110 billion by 2030** (NASSCOM, 2025). The question for enterprise leaders is no longer whether to operate a GCC — it is whether their GCC governance model is designed for the demands of an AI-native era.

## Why GCC governance redesign is a strategic imperative

Legacy GCC operating models were built for execution efficiency: standardized processes, defined SLAs, and predictable output. They were not built for GenAI adoption, advanced analytics, or autonomous decision-making.

The governance gap is significant. According to the EY GCC Pulse Survey 2025:

> **83%** of GCCs are already engaging with GenAI, and **58%** are actively developing agentic AI capabilities — yet only **7%** have established a dedicated Center of Excellence to manage risk and governance.
> — *EY GCC Pulse Survey, 2025*

Most centers are scaling AI capability faster than their governance frameworks can absorb it.

This creates compounding risk: fragmented data strategies, ungoverned AI deployment, misaligned incentive structures between GCC leadership and global headquarters, and talent models built for yesterday's delivery paradigm.

> Compliance complexity and data privacy concerns within GCCs rose from **32% to 42%** in a single year — a direct consequence of accelerating AI adoption without proportionate governance investment.
> — *EY GCC Pulse Survey, 2025*

Redesigning GCC governance is not an administrative exercise. It is the foundational enabler for everything that comes next.

---

## The three pillars of a future-ready GCC governance framework

### 1. AI governance embedded into the operating model

In a GenAI-first GCC, governance cannot be a compliance overlay. It must be structural. This means establishing explainability standards for AI-driven decisions, human-in-the-loop controls for high-stakes workflows, and continuous monitoring frameworks that surface model drift, data quality issues, and regulatory exposure in real time.

The EY Intelligent GCC solution suite identifies **responsible AI governance** as one of four non-negotiable pillars for AI-native centers — alongside architecture design, talent development, and value chain redesign. Organizations that treat AI governance as a post-deployment concern consistently underperform those that embed it at the design stage.

Effective AI governance frameworks for GCCs define role clarity between AI workflow architects, agent supervisors, and responsible AI leads — ensuring that autonomous systems operate with **accountability, not just efficiency**.

### 2. Analytics-led delivery as the new performance standard

The transition from activity-based metrics to outcome-based performance is one of the defining characteristics of high-maturity GCCs.

> Business intelligence adoption among GCCs increased from **80% to 86%** between 2024 and 2025, while data strategy adoption rose from **51% to 67%** — reflecting a sector-wide recognition that analytics capability is now a core delivery competency, not a support function.
> — *EY GCC Pulse Survey, 2025*

Governance redesign must formalize this shift: establishing unified data platforms, defining data ownership and lineage standards, and creating cross-functional analytics forums that connect GCC insights directly to enterprise decision-making cycles.

> **87%** of GCCs now own end-to-end global processes, and **45%** actively participate in global decision-making — a level of integration that demands analytics infrastructure capable of generating board-ready intelligence, not operational dashboards.
> — *EY, 2025*

### 3. Innovation governance as a scalable discipline

Unstructured innovation produces pilots. Governed innovation produces scale.

> **67%** of GCCs are now creating dedicated innovation teams and incubation programs to generate, test, and globalize ideas — but the differentiator between centers that commercialize innovation and those that accumulate proof-of-concepts is governance maturity.
> — *EY GCC Pulse Survey, 2025*

An effective GCC innovation governance framework defines:

- **Intake and prioritization** processes for new ideas
- **Stage-gate criteria** for investment decisions
- **IP ownership and commercialization** pathways
- **Feedback loops** between GCC innovation labs and global product and strategy teams

Without this structure, innovation remains a cultural aspiration rather than a business outcome.

---

## Talent and technology: the enabling layer

GCC governance redesign fails without parallel investment in talent and technology infrastructure. Reskilling initiatives have grown to **71% of GCCs in 2025**, with emerging roles — AI governance architects, agentic AI workflow designers, responsible AI validators — becoming standard capability requirements rather than specialist additions.

On the technology side, GRC platforms, unified data lakes, and agentic AI orchestration layers are increasingly the infrastructure through which governance is operationalized.

> By 2026, AI tools are expected to automate up to **80% of routine operational tasks** within GCCs — shifting human capital toward complex design, IP development, and strategic oversight.
> — *Gratuity Consulting, 2026*

These are roles that require governance structures, not just technical skills.

---

## The governance imperative for 2025 and beyond

The GCC transformation journey — from execution engine to AI-driven innovation hub — is well underway.

> **92%** of GCC leaders affirm that GCCs now contribute far beyond cost arbitrage.
> — *EY, 2025*

But the distance between aspiration and durable capability is governance.

Organizations that redesign their GCC governance frameworks for AI-native operations, analytics-led delivery, and structured innovation will build centers that don't just support enterprise strategy — **they will shape it.**
