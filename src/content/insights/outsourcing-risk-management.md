## Is Your Outsourcing Risk Truly Governed?

A structured framework for identifying, assessing, and continuously managing vendor and third-party outsourcing risks — aligned to **RBI's Master Direction on Outsourcing of IT Services**, SEBI's outsourcing norms, and sector-specific regulatory mandates.

Not all vendors carry equal risk. The RBI's risk-based approach requires that **material outsourcing arrangements** receive heightened due diligence, contractual controls, and continuous oversight proportionate to their criticality.

### Outsourcing Risk Exposure Hierarchy

*Higher criticality = stricter controls + more frequent oversight.*

1. **Material Outsourcing (Critical)**
2. **High-Risk Third Parties**
3. **Standard Vendors**
4. **Low-Risk / Ancillary Suppliers**

### Risk Classification Dimensions

- Criticality to core business operations
- Access to customer data and financial systems
- Regulatory and reputational exposure
- Vendor's financial and operational resilience

### Vendor Risk Tiers

| Tier | Scope | Controls |
|---|---|---|
| **Material Outsourcing** | Core & regulated functions — IT infrastructure, payment processing, cloud services, core banking support | RBI prior approval may apply. Board-level oversight mandatory. |
| **High-Risk** | Vendors accessing customer PII, financial records, or critical systems | Enhanced due diligence and contractual protections required. |
| **Standard** | Operational support — HR platforms, marketing tools, non-critical SaaS | Standard due diligence and periodic review. |
| **Low-Risk** | Ancillary / commodity services — facilities, stationery, generic utilities | Basic vendor registration and annual sign-off sufficient. |

> **Unlimited regulatory sanctions** under the RBI Act for non-compliance with outsourcing directions — including business restriction orders and licence conditions.

## When Outsourcing Has No Owner, No Controls, No Trail

> "Who approved this arrangement, what risks were assessed, and how are you monitoring vendor performance and compliance today?"

Without a structured outsourcing risk framework, your organisation cannot answer the regulator's most fundamental question.

Outsourcing risk is not a procurement matter. It is a **continuous regulatory discipline**, embedded in how vendors are selected, contracted, monitored, and exited.

### Framework Components

1. **Outsourcing Inventory & Materiality Assessment** — Maintain a live register of all outsourced functions. Classify each by materiality, regulatory sensitivity, and data access. RBI requires this as a minimum baseline.
2. **Vendor Due Diligence & Risk Scoring** — Conduct structured pre-engagement due diligence covering financial health, operational resilience, information security posture, regulatory standing, and sub-contractor dependencies.
3. **Contractual Protections & SLA Architecture** — Every material outsourcing agreement must include RBI-mandated clauses: audit rights, data localisation, business continuity obligations, exit provisions, and regulator access rights.
4. **Ongoing Monitoring & Performance Review** — Periodic vendor performance assessments, control testing, incident tracking, and sub-contractor oversight — documented and available for regulatory inspection.
5. **Exit Planning & Concentration Risk Management** — Board-approved exit strategies for every material vendor. Concentration risk assessment where a single vendor supports multiple critical functions.

### Five Failure Modes Without a Framework

1. **No Materiality Assessment → All Vendors Treated Equally.** Critical outsourcing arrangements receive no additional oversight. RBI-defined material arrangements go unidentified, leaving the entity in breach of mandatory reporting and approval obligations.
2. **No Due Diligence → Vendor Risk Imported, Unchecked.** Third-party operational failures, data breaches, and regulatory violations become the regulated entity's liability. The entity inherits risks it never assessed.
3. **No Contractual Controls → No Audit Rights, No Recourse.** Without RBI-mandated contract clauses, the entity cannot exercise inspection rights, enforce data protection obligations, or compel business continuity measures from the vendor.
4. **No Ongoing Monitoring → Stale Risk Picture.** Vendor risk changes after onboarding — financial deterioration, sub-contractor changes, security incidents. Without continuous oversight, the entity discovers failures only after they materialise.
5. **No Exit Strategy → Operational Hostage to Vendor Failure.** When a material vendor fails or is terminated, the absence of a tested exit plan creates operational disruption, customer harm, and regulatory criticism of board governance.

### Regulatory Consequences

- **RBI Inspection Findings** — Formal adverse findings recorded in Risk Assessment Reports during annual inspections.
- **Enforcement Action** — Business restrictions, monetary penalties, and public censure under RBI Act provisions.
- **Board Accountability** — RBI holds Boards directly accountable for material outsourcing governance failures.

## Assess. Contract. Monitor.

A defensible outsourcing risk programme rests on three interdependent disciplines — **Due Diligence** determines vendor fitness, **Contractual Architecture** creates enforceable obligations, and **Continuous Monitoring** ensures those obligations are met and evidenced throughout the relationship lifecycle.

### Pillar 01 — Vendor Due Diligence & Risk Assessment

- Structured pre-engagement questionnaire covering 8 risk domains
- Financial health assessment: audited accounts, credit standing, ownership structure
- Information security and data protection posture review
- Regulatory standing check: RBI-registered, SEBI-compliant, sector-licensed
- Sub-contractor mapping and fourth-party risk identification
- Risk score assigned: Material / High / Standard / Low

### Pillar 02 — Contractual Controls & RBI-Mandated Clauses

- Explicit scope of outsourced activity — no undisclosed sub-contracting
- RBI and regulatory audit access rights — direct inspection entitlement
- Data localisation, sovereignty, and security obligations
- Business continuity and disaster recovery requirements with tested SLAs
- Termination rights, wind-down obligations, and data return provisions
- Liability, indemnity, and incident notification timelines

### Pillar 03 — Ongoing Oversight & Evidence Chain

- Annual vendor risk re-assessment with updated due diligence
- Quarterly performance reviews against contractual SLAs
- Incident and breach register with vendor-attributed events
- Sub-contractor change notification and approval controls
- Concentration risk dashboard — single-vendor dependency mapping
- Audit trail retained for full contract lifecycle plus 5 years

### Outsourcing Governance Maturity Model — Where Does Your Organisation Sit?

| Level | Stage | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Ad Hoc | No vendor register. Outsourcing approved informally. No due diligence documentation. Not RBI inspection-ready. |
| **L2** | Defined | Basic vendor list maintained. Some due diligence conducted. Contracts exist but lack RBI-mandated clauses. Materiality not formally assessed. |
| **L3** | Managed | Materiality framework in place. Due diligence structured. Contracts RBI-compliant. Monitoring periodic but not continuous. Evidence partially captured. |
| **L4** | Optimised | Full outsourcing register live. Risk-tiered oversight active. Real-time monitoring dashboards. Board reporting on concentration and material vendor risk. Defensible under any RBI inspection. |

## Identify. Control. Exit.

Outsourcing risk management does not end at vendor onboarding. It becomes operational through continuous oversight — where vendor performance is tested against contractual obligations, concentration risk is actively managed, and every material arrangement has a board-approved exit strategy ready for execution.

### Outsourcing Register & Materiality Dashboard

- Live inventory of all outsourced functions with materiality classification
- Regulatory approval status tracking — RBI prior approval where required
- Sub-contractor and fourth-party dependency mapping
- Concentration risk flags where a vendor supports multiple critical functions

### Vendor Performance & Control Testing

- Quarterly SLA performance reviews with documented outcomes
- Annual information security and business continuity assessments
- Incident and near-miss register attributed by vendor
- Right-to-audit exercises with findings logged and remediated

### Exit Planning & Concentration Risk Controls

- Board-approved exit strategy for every material vendor
- Tested transition plan: internal fallback or alternative vendor
- Data retrieval, destruction, and portability obligations enforced
- Concentration risk threshold breaches escalated to the Board Risk Committee

### The Outsourcing Risk Lifecycle

1. **Identify** — Map all outsourced functions. Assess materiality. Determine RBI notification and approval obligations before engagement.
2. **Due Diligence** — Conduct structured risk assessment across financial, operational, security, and regulatory dimensions. Score and classify the vendor.
3. **Contract** — Execute an RBI-compliant agreement with the full clause set: audit rights, data protection, BCP, sub-contractor controls, and exit provisions.
4. **Monitor** — Ongoing performance reviews, control testing, incident tracking, sub-contractor oversight, and concentration risk management.
5. **Review & Exit** — Annual re-assessment. Board reporting on material vendors. Tested exit plan maintained and updated throughout the relationship.
