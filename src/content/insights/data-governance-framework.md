## Is Your Data Truly Governed?

Ungoverned data is a silent liability — and structure is the only safeguard.

This bulletin lays out a strategic roadmap for **data classification, retention schedules, ownership accountability, and audit trail architecture** — aligned to India's evolving regulatory landscape.

### Data Classification Hierarchy

- **Restricted — Highest Sensitivity.** PII, health records, legal files, board documents. *Breach triggers regulatory notification.*
- **Confidential — Business Sensitive.** Contracts, HR files, vendor terms, strategy. *Access limited to role-need only.*
- **Internal — Operational Data.** Policies, meeting notes, process docs. *Staff-only; not for external sharing.*
- **Public — Open Information.** Marketing content, published reports, website data. *No access restriction needed.*

> **73%** of organisations have no formal data classification policy, exposing them to unrestricted internal data access.

> **6.8× higher breach cost** when unclassified data is breached, versus organisations with documented retention and ownership controls.

> **₹250 Cr maximum penalty** under the DPDP Act — per violation — for failure to maintain reasonable security safeguards over personal data assets.

## When Data Has No Owner, No Rules, No Trail

> "Show us who had access to this data, why, and for how long."

Without a governance framework, an organisation simply cannot answer that most basic regulatory question — the answer doesn't exist.

Governance is not a one-time project. It is a **continuous operating discipline**, embedded in how data is created, used, stored, and destroyed.

### Five Failure Modes Without Governance

1. **No Classification → Unrestricted Access.** Sensitive PII sits alongside public files; anyone with system access can view, copy, or export it without control.
2. **No Retention Policy → Indefinite Storage.** Data accumulates beyond legal limits; stale personal data becomes a direct liability under DPDP Act S.8(7).
3. **No Ownership → No Accountability.** Incidents go unresolved; no one is responsible when a data subject exercises their rights under law.
4. **No Audit Trail → No Defensibility.** Compliance cannot be demonstrated during a regulatory inquiry, internal investigation, or legal dispute.
5. **No Lineage → Shadow Data Proliferation.** Unknown copies live in vendor systems, email archives, and analyst spreadsheets — all ungoverned.

### Regulatory Consequence

- **₹250 Cr** — per breach violation
- **₹200 Cr** — breach notification failure
- **₹50 Cr** — any other obligation

### The Six Building Blocks of a Governance Framework

1. **Data Inventory & Asset Register** — Know every asset: what you hold, where it lives, who processes it, and under what legal basis.
2. **Classification Policy & Schema** — Formal tiers (Restricted, Confidential, Internal, Public) with labelling, handling rules, and access controls.
3. **Retention & Disposal Schedules** — Documented retention periods per category, automated disposal triggers, and legal hold procedures.
4. **Ownership & Stewardship Matrix** — Named Data Owners, Stewards, and Custodians for every asset, with clear escalation paths for rights requests.
5. **Audit Trail Architecture** — Immutable logs of who accessed what, when, and why — plus change history, export records, and deletion evidence.
6. **Governance Review Cycle** — Quarterly classification reviews, annual ownership attestation, and continuous monitoring against policy drift.

## Structure. Ownership. Evidence.

A functioning Data Governance Framework rests on three interdependent disciplines: **Classification** determines how data is treated, **Ownership** ensures accountability, and **Audit Trails** make that accountability provable.

### Pillar 01 — Data Classification & Inventory

- Formal classification schema (4 tiers minimum)
- Automated labelling at the point of data creation
- Data inventory covering all systems and vendors
- Sensitivity reassessment when data changes
- Classification tied to access-control rules
- DPIA linkage for high-risk data categories

### Pillar 02 — Ownership & Accountability

- **Data Owner** — accountable for policy compliance
- **Data Steward** — day-to-day quality and governance
- **Data Custodian** — technical storage and security
- Ownership matrix mapped to every asset class
- Rights-request responsibility formally assigned
- Annual ownership attestation programme

### Pillar 03 — Audit Trails & Evidence Chain

- Immutable access logs: who, what, when, why
- Change history for all sensitive data assets
- Deletion and disposal records with timestamps
- Export and sharing logs across all systems
- Audit trail retention: 3–7 years per regulation
- Evidence packages ready for regulatory inquiry

### Retention Lifecycle — From Creation to Secure Destruction

1. **Creation** — Classified at entry. Owner assigned. Retention period set by policy.
2. **Active Use** — Access controlled by tier. Every access and change logged in the audit trail.
3. **Archival** — Moved to long-term storage. Access further restricted. Retention clock tracked.
4. **Review** — Retain further via legal hold, or schedule for disposal. Owner must attest.
5. **Disposal** — Secure destruction. Certificate of disposal retained as regulatory evidence.

### Governance Maturity Model — Where Does Your Organisation Sit?

- **L1 — Ad Hoc.** No policy. No ownership. Data handled by individual instinct.
- **L2 — Defined.** Policies exist. Classification partly applied. Ownership informal.
- **L3 — Managed.** Full framework deployed. Audit trails active. Retention enforced.
- **L4 — Optimised.** Continuous monitoring. Board reporting. Defensible under any audit.
